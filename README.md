# Sounds of Reading

Thirty-eight small games for a six-year-old learning to read, spell, write and
count, and to score a jiu-jitsu match, played on an iPad from the home screen
and shared with his school friends.

No accounts, no tracking, no ads, no third-party requests. It works offline
once opened, and nothing ever leaves the device.

## The games

The home screen groups the tiles into four sections, with three of the
coach's picks for today at the top.

### Reading and spelling

| Game | What the child does |
|---|---|
| **Memory Match** | Flips cards to find pairs — a word with its picture, or two words that share a sound. |
| **Bingo** | The app says a word and shows its picture; he finds it written on his card. |
| **Sound Sort** | A word appears with nothing marked. He drags it (or taps a bin) into the right bin, and the sound lights up. |
| **Word Builder** | One part of the word stays put, he swaps the other. Each build is spoken and judged real or silly. |
| **Roll & Read** | Taps a die, reads the words it lands on aloud. A parent taps ✅ or 🔁, and a bar chart builds up. |
| **Real or Silly?** | Reads a word with no help, then decides whether it is a word at all. |
| **Sentence Smash** | Picks a phrase from each of four rows and hears the silly sentence read back. |
| **Same Sound, Two Ways** | Hears a word with its vowel missing and chooses the spelling: ai or ay, oi or oy. |
| **Tricky Words** | Sees a sight word for a moment, then finds it among four lookalikes. |
| **Sound Rocket** | Steers a rocket to catch falling words with the sound and dodge the rest. |
| **Penalty Shootout** | Reads the word to score past the keeper, then hears one and dives to save it. |
| **Pass and Shoot** | Passes along a line of players, one sound each, then shoots at the word he blended. |
| **Be the Commentator** | Reads a football commentary line with expression, records it and hears it back. |
| **Build the Word** | Hears a word and builds it from sound or letter tiles. |
| **Coach Says** | Reads the coach's written instruction — "Put the frog in the box, then tap the hen" — and does it. The pictures carry no words, so reading is the only way to know what to do. |
| **Big Words** | Reads two-syllable words a part at a time (sun·set, rab·bit), then whole, then (Year 2) taps where a word splits. |

### Handwriting

| Game | What the child does |
|---|---|
| **Trace It** | Traces letters in NSW Foundation style: start dot, direction, then on his own. |
| **Tall, Small, Tail** | Sorts letters by where they sit on the lines, then finds every b among b d p q. |

### Maths

| Game | What the child does |
|---|---|
| **Flash Count** | Sees dots for a moment (dice, a ten frame) and says how many. |
| **Off the Bench** | Some players are on the pitch: how many more make 5, 10 or 20? |
| **Scoreboard Sums** | Adds goals scored and takes away goals ruled offside, within 20. |
| **Number Line Penalty** | Kicks the ball to where a number sits on a line from 0–10 up to 0–120, and on to 1000. |
| **Team Buses** | Reads crowds as full buses of ten and loose fans, loads buses, then (Year 2) trains of 100, builds numbers to 999, and sees 34 as 2 tens and 14. |
| **Keepy-Uppy Count** | Fills the gap in a run of touches: counting on and back, by 2s, 5s and 10s, then (Year 2) by 10s from any number, odd numbers, 3s, and back in 10s and 5s. |
| **Training Drills** | Equal groups of balls in hoops and sharing them fairly, then (Year 2) cones in rows and columns, teams of 2, 5 and 10, and how many rows a pile of cones makes. |
| **Half-Time Oranges** | Is the orange (or the pitch) cut into halves, and half of the bibs for each team; then (Year 2) quarters and eighths, of a shape and of a group. |
| **Jump Line** | Makes the jumps himself along a number line to add and take away within 10 and 20, then (Year 2) within 100 with jumps of ten. |
| **Match Clock** | Reads and sets o'clock and half past, and the days of the week in order; then (Year 2) quarter past and quarter to, the months, and the Australian seasons. |
| **Short Hand, Long Hand** | Drags the hands of a clock whose two scales are kept apart — red hours with a shaded "hour zone", blue minutes on their own ring — then sets the clock to a time and reads one, from o'clock up to any minute. |
| **Fan Survey** | Counts fans waving flags, reads tally marks and a picture graph; then (Year 2) reads a column graph and says how many more. |
| **Fact Family Formation** | Three players are one family (3, 5, 8): turns a fact around and takes it back within 10 and 20; then (Year 2) finds a missing number, and does it with tens to 100. |
| **Kit and Ball Shapes** | Names flat shapes, counts sides and corners, and names the solid objects of football (the ball is a sphere, the cone a cone); then (Year 2) pentagons and hexagons, and whether a kit is the same on both sides. |
| **Coach's Whiteboard** | Left or right of the keeper, then follows moves on a grid (2 squares up, then 3 left); then (Year 2) quarter and half turns, and tells a flip from a slide from a turn. |
| **Ice Cream Van** | Which Australian coin is worth more (the $2 is smaller than the 50c), which coin pays exactly; then (Year 2) a handful of mixed coins, paying the exact price from his purse, and change from a note. |

### Jiu-jitsu 🥋

In English or Portuguese, switched with the 🇦🇺 and 🇧🇷 flags, mid-question too.

| Game | What the child does |
|---|---|
| **Ref's Call** | Sees a move (takedown, sweep, mount…) and calls its IBJJF points, reads the move's name alone, finds the move worth 3; then (Year 2) says whether the referee gives points, an advantage or a penalty. |
| **Match Maths** | Adds up a match move by move (takedown + mount = 2 + 4 = 6), says how far ahead a fighter is and which move made the score; then (Year 2) a whole match, and a draw settled on advantages and penalties. |
| **Weigh-In** | Which side of the balance is heavier, measuring in blocks; then (Year 2) reads the kilograms off the scale, finds his weight class, and adds or takes off the gi. |
| **Ref's Signals** | Reads the referee's hands — fingers for points, an open hand for an advantage, a fist for a penalty; then (Year 2) plays referee, and learns the calls shouted at every competition: Combate! Parou! Lute! |

The maths and jiu-jitsu games follow the NSW Mathematics K–10 syllabus from
Kindergarten to Year 2, and each climbs its own levels: up after three right
in a row, down after two wrong. The Year 2 levels sit at the top of each
ladder. Each round waits for a tap on Start, so nothing is asked while the
page is still loading. Coach Says and Big Words climb the same way.

Finishing a round in most games wins a sticker: a reading game's is for the
sound it practised most, and a maths or jiu-jitsu game's is the game's own.
The stickers collect in a book on the home screen. There is nothing to lose —
every game is completable and no answer is ever marked with a red X.

## The grown-ups corner

The settings are on their own page, so a child tapping about cannot change
his week: hold **⚙ Grown-ups** at the bottom of the home screen for three
seconds. It has this week's sounds and levels, how it is going (the sounds
that are going well and the ones that need practice, and the step reached in
each maths and jiu-jitsu game), Pro mode, the coach, speech and sound effects,
the reading voice, and a fresh sticker book.

## The sounds

The school's eight-level program, from `s a t p i n` up to `aw air are ear eer
ore dge tch`, plus `ph` from the class sound sheet. 78 sounds a child can
practise, about 1,000 real words, 700 silly ones, 50 word families, 170
sentence phrases, 135 tricky words and 60 two-syllable words.

Levels are cumulative, so every word carries the level a child needs to decode
it — not just the level of the sound it practises. "phone" practises `ph` from
level 4, but its split digraph `o_e` is level 7, so it is a level 7 word and a
level 4 child never meets it. Any combination of levels can be chosen, for
every game in the grown-ups corner, or for one session behind a game's ⚙.

## Running it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev              # http://localhost:5173
npm run build            # typecheck, then build to dist/
npm run validate:content # the content test on its own
npm run test:unit        # unit tests for the shared code
npm test                 # the content and unit tests, then Playwright
```

`dist/` is a static site. It has no backend and no build-time configuration,
and every path in it is relative, so it can be dropped on GitHub Pages,
Cloudflare Pages, Netlify or a folder without changing anything.

## Deploying

`.github/workflows/deploy.yml` publishes to GitHub Pages after every push to
`main` — but only once the CI workflow for that push has passed: the content
test, the unit tests, the typecheck and build, and the browser tests. It
deploys exactly the commit CI tested, and runs the content test once more
before it does, so a bad word cannot reach a child.

Set up once: **Settings → Pages → Source → GitHub Actions**. The app is at
`https://eduardopicado.github.io/sounds-of-reading/` and every merge to `main`
updates it.

The URL is public but unlisted — there is no login, because there is nothing
to log in to and nothing stored anywhere but the child's own device. Share it
with the school friends as it is.

To add it to an iPad: open the URL in Safari, tap Share, then **Add to Home
Screen**. It then runs full screen with no browser bar, and keeps working with
no signal. A new version arrives the next time it is opened: close it from the
app switcher and open it again.

## The content file

Everything the games say lives in `src/content/words.ts`. Adding a word means
editing that one file:

```ts
words: 'ship 🚢 | fish 🐟 | shed 🏚 | brush 🪥 | shark 🦈 @6'
```

The emoji is optional. `@6` says the word needs level 6 to decode.

The app has to know exactly which letters to underline. Usually it works that
out, because the sound's spelling appears once. When it appears twice, the word
says which one it means:

```ts
'ba[th] 🛁'            underline the th in bath, not the one that isn't there
'the whole [wh]eelbarrow'   wheelbarrow, not whole, which is /h/
'c[a]k[e] 🎂'          a split digraph is two pieces, both underlined
```

A word where the spelling appears twice with no brackets **fails the content
test** rather than being guessed at. That is deliberate: guessing with
`indexOf` is how the prototypes came to underline "in *the* bath".

## The content test

```sh
npm run validate:content
```

It checks that every real word is a real English word, that no silly word
secretly is one, that every Word Builder build comes out the way it is labelled
both ways round, that nothing is on the blocklist, that no word is both real
and silly, that every grapheme really sits where the file says it does, that
every pair of real words that sound the same with an Australian accent is
listed (Penalty Shootout must never offer a word's twin), and that both tones
of every sound colour clear WCAG AA.

This is what holds the handover's section 8 fixed: *vain*, *zip*, *ding*,
*zing*, *gone*, *zone*, *vice*, *mink*, *gale*, *vat*, *thatch*, *mace*,
*mage* and *vale* are real words and are treated as such; *thong* is out of the
`-ong` silly list; *shink* is out of `-ink`; and the silly words that had no
target sound in them (*zuck*, *vhale*, *zhen*) or could not be pronounced at
all (*vquid*, *glquz*, *glwhi*, *drwhe*) are gone. Below level 4 a silly word
only uses letters the child has actually been taught.

`tools/regen-silly.ts` rebuilds those lists when a sound is added, proposing
only non-words that are pronounceable and contain the grapheme.

## The reading voice

Speech uses the browser's own engine, so nothing is fetched and it works with
no signal. Which voice you get is up to the device. Apple ships each voice in
tiers, told apart by identifier rather than name, so a device can list two
voices called Karen that sound nothing alike:

```
com.apple.voice.super-compact.en-AU.Karen   cut down, for low memory
com.apple.voice.compact.en-AU.Karen         the standard voice
com.apple.voice.enhanced.en-AU.Karen        clearer, a download in Settings
com.apple.voice.premium.en-AU.Karen         clearest, a download in Settings
```

The app ranks by accent first — an Australian child is learning Australian
vowels — then by tier, then by whether the voice works offline, and the
grown-ups corner has a picker listing everything on offer, with a Try it
button.

What a web page is offered is not what Settings has installed. On the iPad
this was built for, Safari offered the page only the super-compact Karen,
however many better English voices had been downloaded, and Siri's voices are
never available to the web at all. So downloading a voice is worth trying but
may change nothing; `#/voices` (linked from nowhere) lists exactly what the
browser offers, English and Portuguese, with each voice's tier and the one the
app uses. The Portuguese commentator picks its own voice the same way, and
can sound much better than the English one.

The app can also play recordings of the words instead, made ahead of time by
`npm run make:audio` (see `public/audio/README.md`). None ship yet: the
generator has no text-to-speech service wired up, so every word is spoken by
the device.

## Design rules

- **Colour identifies a sound; it never carries text.** Letters stay dark ink
  and the sound colour becomes a thick underline plus a translucent wash
  behind them. Sentence Smash shipped mustard-on-cream letters once and was
  unreadable.
- Andika throughout for anything the child reads, for its single-storey **a**
  and **g** — the letterforms on the school sheet. Self-hosted, latin subset,
  about 25 KB for both weights.
- Feedback is kind: a shake for wrong, a bounce for right, no red X, no losing.
  Every game is completable.
- 48px minimum tap targets, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, rounds of two
  to five minutes.
- Sound effects are synthesised with WebAudio rather than loaded, so there is
  nothing to fetch and nothing to cache. Both speech and effects can be turned
  off in the grown-ups corner.
- Leaving a game stops everything it started: its timers, its speech and its
  sound effects (see `src/lib/life.ts`).

## Tests

- `tests/content.spec.ts` — the content test above.
- `tests/unit/` — the shared code in Node with small fakes: speech and the
  voice ranking, recorded clips, sound effects, timers, the coach, saved
  settings, and the maths and jiu-jitsu questions.
- `tests/e2e/` — Playwright: loading, a full round of each game, changing
  settings, the win screen, the sticker book, offline after first load, speech
  falling back to `en-AU`, leaving a game mid-sentence, and the app surviving
  both blocked `localStorage` and a browser where touching `speechSynthesis`
  throws. Every test asserts zero console errors and no request to any other
  domain.
- `tests/e2e/a11y.spec.ts` — axe against WCAG 2.1 AA on every screen.

They run on three viewports: an iPad at 820×1180 and 1180×820, and a 375px
phone.

Lighthouse is not a dependency, but the build is kept clean against it:

```sh
npm run build && npx vite preview --port 4173 &
npx lighthouse http://localhost:4173/ --only-categories=accessibility,best-practices,performance
```
